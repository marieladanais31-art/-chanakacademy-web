EDUCATIONAL MODEL

What is a Chanak Microschool?

A Chanak Microschool is a small in-person learning community (typically 5 to 30 students) combining the warmth, adaptability, and healthy peer socialization of a local cohort with the accredited academic structure and official standing of an American private school.

🏛️

Who is it for?

Schools, churches, organizations, and organized family groups (for example, 10 or more families) seeking an in-person academic program with official U.S. credentials.

👥

Small Cohorts

Individualized attention where every learner is known by name, learning style, and personal strengths, moving beyond the crowding of conventional classrooms.

🎓

Accredited Standing

Backing from Chanak International Academy (FLDOE #134620), with Accredited by MSA-CESS, issuing official transcripts and an American High School Diploma track.

🌟

Routine & Socialization

Structured daily cadence with collaborative inquiry, group discussions, character development, and meaningful peer friendships in a safe setting.

The three pillars

What makes a Chanak Microschool possible

A Microschool is a partnership with a community of families that already exists (launch minimum: 10 families), in the United States and in other countries. Every site rests on the same three pillars.

A · American curriculum accredited by MSA-CESS, provided by Chanak

American K–12 curriculum aligned with Florida’s B.E.S.T. standards and rules, under Chanak’s MSA-CESS accreditation. Includes Mastery Learning, the 60/20/20 model, platform, records and diploma. You may complement it with your own curriculum within the Chanak framework.

B · Facilities that meet municipal and state requirements

The space must comply with the municipal, regional or national rules of its country: permitted use, safety, accessibility, insurance and capacity. The partner is legally responsible for its facilities; Chanak provides a checklist and guides you.

C · Teaching staff qualified under the country’s laws and certified by Chanak

Your team meets your country’s qualification and background-check requirements, and is also certified through Chanak’s training (Chanak TrainUp) to apply Mastery Learning and the 60/20/20 model.

Chanak guides and accompanies you, but does not replace local legal advice and does not guarantee authorizations, school codes or public funds: each country has its own requirements.

Are you a family and not leading a community? Your way in is Guided Homeschool or Dual Diploma; we will gladly guide you.

The middle ground

Neither alone at home nor a large school

Chanak comes alongside each family in three approaches with the same standard: homeschool, the local school with Dual Diploma and, in between, the Microschool: a small community learning together, with structure and personal attention.

  • Your community brings the families, the space and its identity
  • Chanak brings the academic framework, platform, training and records
  • Partners may use materials compatible with their approach
HOLISTIC PEDAGOGY

How Your Child Learns

Our instructional design centers on each learner through four structural pillars crafted for conceptual understanding and character development.

🎯

Mastery Learning

Students advance only when they have demonstrated comprehensive mastery of foundational concepts. No hidden gaps accumulate; learners proceed at their natural pace.

📐

The 60/20/20 Model

A balanced curricular framework: 60% Core U.S. Curriculum under accredited standards, 20% Local Extension (heritage language, history, geography), and 20% Life Skills, character, and worldview.

🧭

Chanak-Certified Coordinator

Each cohort is facilitated on-site by a trained coordinator certified by Chanak, equipped in student mentoring, adaptive pacing, learning atmosphere, and individual follow-up.

💻

Integrated SIS Platform

Chanak's Student Information System (SIS) centralizes the student's individualized educational plan (IEP), weekly goals, assessment outcomes, and official school records.

TUITION & FUNDING

How Tuition is Structured

Funding pathways depend on your country of residence and the participation framework chosen by your family.

🇺🇸 United States: State Scholarship Funds (ESA / EFA)

In states where Chanak participates as an authorized education service provider (e.g., Florida Step Up PEP/FES-UA, Utah Fits All, Arizona ClassWallet, New Hampshire EFA), families may apply their state education scholarship accounts toward eligible services.

Important Program Notice: In-person services currently in the approval process for each program. Funding eligibility and authorized use depend on specific state program guidelines and scholarship administrator policies.

🌐 International: Local Center Fee

In Spain, Mexico, Panama, and other regions, families remit tuition directly to the local partner learning center managing physical facilities and on-site staff. Chanak provides the accredited U.S. curriculum, digital platform, and official school transcripts.

To view direct enrollment tuition and distance-learning fees for fully virtual tracks, please visit our homepage tuition section.

Reference

Legal framework by country

Spain

Reference rules

  • Real Decreto 806/1993, de 28 de mayo, sobre régimen de centros docentes extranjeros en España
  • Código Técnico de la Edificación (Real Decreto 314/2006): seguridad en caso de incendio (DB-SI) y accesibilidad (DB-SUA)

A centre that teaches a foreign curriculum needs authorization as a foreign school from the competent education authority. For Spanish students, Spanish Language and Culture teaching is included; requirements vary by autonomous community.

Panama

Reference rules

  • Ley 47 de 1946, Orgánica de Educación (texto único aprobado por el Decreto Ejecutivo 305 de 2004, Gaceta Oficial 25042)

Opening a private centre is processed with the Ministry of Education (MEDUCA). The U.S. curriculum is combined with the national core subjects required by the authority.

Mexico

Reference rules

  • Constitución Política de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos, artículo 3.º
  • Ley General de Educación (DOF 30-09-2019)
  • Acuerdo número 286 de la SEP sobre revalidación y equivalencia de estudios (DOF 30-10-2000, última reforma DOF 18-04-2017)

U.S. studies may be submitted for revalidation or equivalency with the SEP, with the documentation the authority requires. That rule governs recognition of each student’s studies and does not replace the centre’s own authorization (RVOE): the legal pathway must be confirmed with local counsel before opening.

Guatemala

Reference rules

  • Ley de Educación Nacional (Decreto Legislativo 12-91)

Studies completed in a foreign program may be submitted for equivalency with the Ministry of Education (MINEDUC) under the procedure in force.

El Salvador

Reference rules

  • Ley General de Educación (Decreto Legislativo 917 de 1996), artículos 59 a 62 sobre equivalencias de estudios realizados en el extranjero

Recognition of studies completed abroad is handled by the Ministry of Education under the procedure in force.

Ecuador

Reference rules

  • Ley Orgánica de Educación Intercultural (LOEI, Registro Oficial 417 de 31-03-2011) y sus reformas

Ministry rules on homeschooling and distance learning have been reformed several times between 2023 and 2026. Families’ link to an international program and study equivalencies must be confirmed with the Ministry of Education before it is offered.

Colombia

Reference rules

  • Ley 115 de 1994 (Ley General de Educación)
  • Decreto 1075 de 2015 (Decreto Único Reglamentario del Sector Educación)
  • Resolución 24302 de 2021 del Ministerio de Educación Nacional (convalidación de estudios de preescolar, básica y media realizados en el exterior)

Studies completed abroad may be submitted for validation with the Ministry of National Education under the procedure in force; requirements and conditions are checked case by case.

Costa Rica

Reference rules

  • Ley Fundamental de Educación (Ley 2160 de 1957)
  • Ley 7600, de Igualdad de Oportunidades para las Personas con Discapacidad

Recognition and equivalency of foreign studies are decided by the Ministry of Public Education (MEP), which applies restrictive conditions to foreign programs completed inside the country. Each case is reviewed before it is offered to families.

United States

State and municipal rules where the site is located.

The Microschool operates under Chanak’s MSA-CESS accreditation and Florida’s B.E.S.T. standards, without its own school code. ESA funds apply only in states where Chanak is an approved provider and only to the services approved in each program.

Reference information, not legal advice. Requirements change and vary by region; verify them with your country’s education authority. Last reviewed: November 17, 2026.

How a Chanak Microschool opens

Six guided steps. You do not have to invent anything from scratch, but you do lead with responsibility.

1

First meeting

We get to know your community, your why and the families who already trust you.

2

Application and feasibility

Together we review legal and financial feasibility in your country or state.

3

Partnership agreement

We sign the framework agreement and the annex of authorized programs.

4

Training and certification

Your coordinator and mentors train and certify with Chanak.

5

Diagnostics and student onboarding

Each student takes a diagnostic and joins Chanak’s student system.

6

Opening and quality

We open with follow-up, regular meetings and quality reviews.

Two geographies, different requirements

United States: in states where Chanak is an approved provider, families may request the use of their ESA funds under each program’s rules.

International: each country has its own requirements with its education authority. Your entity obtains local authorizations and Chanak provides the academic framework.

Signing an agreement is not a government authorization and does not automatically extend accreditation to a site: each implementation is validated and supervised.

Five keys before you start

What most helps a Microschool start healthy and stay sustainable.

Start with why

Before curriculum and furniture: which need of your families do you want to meet? Write it in one sentence.

Define your ideal family

A message for everyone speaks to no one. Describe their values, goals and what they cannot find today.

Structure before enthusiasm

Your own legal entity, a separate bank account, insurance and child safeguarding before charging fees or signing a lease.

Budget and break-even

Add up monthly costs and divide by tuition: you will know how many students you need to be sustainable. Review it monthly.

Relationships before ads

Small meet-the-founder sessions, short videos about your why and organized follow-up with every interested family.
