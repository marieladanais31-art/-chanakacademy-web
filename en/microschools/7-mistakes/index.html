7 mistakes that cost a Microschool (and how to avoid them from day one)

Opening a Microschool is an act of faith and also of responsibility. This guide gathers what most helps a small community start healthy and stay sustainable, organized around Chanak’s three pillars.

1

Starting with the “what” instead of the “why”

It is tempting to begin by choosing tables, schedules and books. But a community that does not know why it exists scatters at the first difficulty.

How to avoid it: Write your mission and the worldview behind it in one sentence. Share it with your first families and let it guide every later decision.

2

Trying to serve everyone

A message for everyone speaks to no one. Without a clear profile, each family expects something different and the team wears out trying to please them all.

How to avoid it: Describe your community: its values, goals and what it cannot find today. A realistic starting point is about 10 families who already trust you.

3

Leaving the legal framework for last

Each country has its own requirements for opening a space with minors and for recognizing studies. Discovering them after signing or charging is costly.

How to avoid it: First settle the legal entity, registration and whatever authorization your country requires, with local counsel. Do not sign contracts or charge tuition before then.

4

Neglecting the facilities

A pleasant space is not necessarily a suitable one. Permitted use, safety, accessibility and insurance are the partner’s responsibility.

How to avoid it: Before committing, check the permitted use, fire safety, accessibility, capacity and liability insurance (Pillar B).

5

Hiring without certifying

Goodwill is not enough to walk with a group of students. Without qualifications and shared training, every adult teaches in a different way.

How to avoid it: Secure the qualifications and background checks your country requires, and complete Chanak’s certification before the first class (Pillar C).

6

Setting tuition out of fear and never updating the budget

Tuition set too low to “fill seats” leaves the school without margin. And a budget made once and filed away stops describing reality.

How to avoid it: Work out your break-even with the calculator, set tuition with the numbers in front of you and review the budget every month.

7

Recruiting only with ads and no follow-up

Families do not entrust their children to an ad, but to people. Without follow-up, interest cools within days.

How to avoid it: Put relationships first: small gatherings, real conversations and a simple record of each family in three steps, Interest → Conversation → Commitment.

You do not have to do it alone

Chanak provides Pillar A (the accredited curriculum) and guides you on the other two. You bring the community, the space and the team.

  • A · American curriculum accredited by MSA-CESS, provided by Chanak
  • B · Facilities that meet municipal and state requirements
  • C · Teaching staff qualified under the country’s laws and certified by Chanak

Chanak guides and accompanies you, but does not replace local legal advice and does not guarantee authorizations, school codes or public funds: each country has its own requirements.
